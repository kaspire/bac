Reading the numbers — honest version

Two real axes. Hand-number and hands-from-end are independent physical positions. Row is ((hand−1) mod rows)+1 — a display wrap with a fake period, no physics, ignore any pattern in it. Column is just the hand binned, so the "by hand number" table already covers it.

Density vs baseline. A fresh-shoe pair lands ~14.3% of hands (either side). Each band shows observed density and a z versus that. |z|<2 is noise; you need a band that stays high across many shoes before trusting it.

The one real effect. A depleted shoe runs slightly uneven, which nudges pair odds up near the end — so a faint rise in the last bands is physically plausible. But it's small, and "4 hits in 14 hands at the end" of one shoe is also exactly what ordinary clustering produces. Only the pooled density across dozens of shoes can tell the difference.

Power. ~30+ shoes before the end-bands mean much; ~100 before you'd trust a 1–2 point density edge. The cut card stops shoes ~14 cards from the true end, so the very deepest depletion never gets dealt — keep that in mind for the last band.

Reminder it can't beat the edge. Even a real density bump in one band doesn't overcome the 10–14% house edge on the bet. This finds your fastest-hitting window for extract-and-leave timing — not an edge over the game.
